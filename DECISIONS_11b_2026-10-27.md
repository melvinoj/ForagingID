## C. Genus / family sweep

**Which fills wrote the wrong values (evidence in the DB, not a guess):**

1. **Family column holding a genus (or a section/subgenus)** — the Wikidata fill. `_apply_wikidata_to_species` writes `r.family` into an empty family (app/services/enrichment.py:880-881) from the SPARQL `familyLabel` (app/integrations/wikidata.py:259-263). Every one of these cards has a stored Wikidata response (enrichment_sources) whose familyLabel is exactly the bad value (e.g. 225 on 1 Jun 12:43: familyLabel "Imleria", entity Q19286081 = the genus). Stored responses: May 133/133 and Jun 350/380 returned a non-family label; the last genus-as-family response is 5 Jun 22:57 (416 "Carpinus"). The query today requires rank = family (P105 Q35409, wikidata.py:73-74 and 189-190), so this writer is **no longer live** — but the parser still has no "-aceae" check, so a family item whose English label is a common name ("Orchid", "cactus" returned in Jul) would still be written into an empty family.
2. **Genus/family of a different organism** (Viola as Apocynaceae/Vinca, Acer as Oleaceae/Fraxinus, puffball as Caprifoliaceae/Dipsacus) — copied from another candidate on the photo that created the card. Each one matches the family/genus of a non-matching candidate on its first photo, and the card was created at the moment Melvin corrected that photo (e.g. 121 Viola riviniana: obs 8235 Vinca minor → Viola riviniana 27 May; 157 Fistulina: obs 8375 Pararchidendron → Fistulina 28 May, card created 34 ms after the edit). All 27 May – 5 Jun. The code that did it is **not in the current tree** (git history restarts 6 Jul): today's correction path creates a bare card (app/services/species_link.py:225), and `_backfill_taxonomy_from_observations` only uses a candidate whose name matches the card (app/services/enrichment.py:801-803). One live way to get the same mismatch remains: a rename keeps the old family/genus (app/api/culinary.py:973-975) — 160 *Boletus edulis* was renamed from the typo *Boletus edulus* and kept the Calluna values it already had.
3. **Family differs from GBIF only** — PlantNet's family at card creation (app/api/scan.py:2112-2113, e.g. 412/413 Aspleniaceae, 781 Celastraceae, 430/477 Polypodiaceae) or a different taxonomic opinion. Not a bug.

## G. Junk cards

Every card with no live photo — orphan-marked or not (87; 653 *Oenanthe crocata* left out, kept as a reference card). A delete removes the card and all its species-keyed rows; its rejected photos stay (species_id → NULL, species_primary kept as history).

I split the list, because "no live photo" also catches real European species. Deleting those loses their enrichment and, for a toxic one, takes it out of the hazard index (load_hazard_index reads every toxic/deadly card, app/services/hazard_candidates.py:71) — so a photo listing *Viscum album* would stop getting its hazard line. **No toxic or deadly card is deleted by default.**

# Fireweed Shoots with Lemon, Pine, and Soft Cheese

There’s a brief window in early spring—maybe three weeks, maybe less—when fireweed shoots push through the soil and taste like something worth getting your hands dirty for. Before the stem fibres toughen and that astringent, soapy character takes hold, the young shoots are tender, faintly sweet, with a greenness that carries the memory of ash and regrowth. This dish treats the shoot like what it is: a seasonal moment. You can scale it from a single plate to a tray for eight, and it works as a starter, a light lunch, or alongside grilled fish or lamb.

**Ingredients**  
For two as a starter:  
- 200g fireweed shoots, harvested when no taller than your hand, tips still curled  
- 1 lemon  
- A small handful of young pine needles or a few sprigs of pine-infused fir  
- 100g soft goat’s cheese or a young sheep’s milk curd  
- Good olive oil  
- Flaky salt, black pepper  
- Optional: a few fireweed flowers (in season) or early blossoms from the same patch

**Method**

Pick your shoots when the ground is still cold and the days are stretching. Cut them close to the soil line, discarding any that feel woody or have begun to leaf out fully. Rinse them well—fireweed colonises disturbed ground, and ash or grit clings to the base. Pat dry.

Bring a shallow pan of salted water to a rolling boil. Drop the shoots in for ninety seconds—no longer. The colour will deepen from pale green to a vivid, almost luminous jade. Drain immediately and spread on a cloth. Don’t shock them in ice water; you want them to carry residual warmth.

Lay the warm shoots on a plate or a wooden board. Crumble the cheese over them in irregular pieces—not too fine, you want texture. Spoon the lemon-oil dressing over the top. Scatter the pine needles or fir tips like you mean it, not for show but because the resinous bite cuts through the cheese and lifts the vegetal note of the shoots.

Finish with a last turn of pepper and a few drops of olive oil from height. If you have flowers, tuck them in now. Serve immediately, with good bread to catch the dressing and the crumbles of cheese that fall to the plate.

**A variation**

If you have access to young fireweed roots—and you’re willing to dig—try this: scrub the roots clean, slice them thinly, and boil them in two changes of water until tender.
